A book born from its own epistemic failure

The investigation begins with two observations about a child. An unwanted app or disliked food may feel as though it should be removed rather than ignored. A stream of short videos, by contrast, can hold attention through repeated opportunities for a better next item. Asked whether the situations shared a connection, a model acknowledged their differences and then built an elegant common theory around the cost of ignoring. Aversion became the cost of tolerating; continued viewing became the cost of giving up a possible reward.

The user then revealed the question as an epistemic trap. General vocabulary such as attention, control, salience, and cost can describe both scenes without establishing a shared mechanism. The resulting manuscript had not invented a conspicuously false fact; it had converted analogies into explanatory structure. Its sentences remained locally plausible while the document’s architecture became globally confident. This subtle failure motivates the book: how does a generative system manufacture the form of a theory before producing the evidence or discriminating test that earns unification?

A working hypothesis that may not deserve to survive

The proposed term is coherence pressure: a tendency in generation, selection, or evaluation to favor representations that close discursive gaps and produce unity, even when closure requires unsupported bridges, diminished distinctions, or the conversion of unresolved plurality into one narrative. The definition is behavioral. It does not claim that a model feels discomfort about incoherence, nor does it posit a dedicated internal circuit. Several forces may combine: autoregressive continuation, task completion, evaluator preference, user framing, conversational consistency, and decoding choices.

The phrase is not presented as an established scientific construct, and the book disclaims priority for it. Related recent uses do not supply one standardized definition or mechanism. The case may ultimately be explained by neighboring concepts such as sycophancy, false-premise acceptance, framing, coverage failure, or ordinary human story construction. The programme is justified only if it identifies stable behaviors and consequential effects beyond those alternatives. This self-suspicion is not decorative modesty; it determines the experiments and abandonment conditions proposed later.

Explanatory bridges and four kinds of coherence

The book separates logical, discourse, explanatory, and narrative coherence. A text may avoid contradiction yet explain little. It may be beautifully organized and factually wrong. A false theory may fit selectively represented evidence. A narrative may turn contingency into apparent inevitability. Coherence is indispensable to understanding, but dangerous when it operates both as a production objective and as a cue readers use to judge truth. Fluent form can lend epistemic authority to relations that the source never established.

The key unit is the explanatory bridge: a relation added between components that were separate, merely correlated, analogous, or underidentified in the source. A bridge may assert common mechanism, causality, subsumption, complementarity, analogy, or reconciliation. It can be explicit, supported, exploratory, unmarked speculation, or contradicted. Bridges are not intrinsically wrong; scientific advance often depends on them. The failure occurs when a fertile possibility receives the status of a supported relation without alternatives, provenance, scope, and a test capable of making it lose.

How a plausible theory gets manufactured

The case develops through a recognizable sequence. Juxtaposition in the prompt becomes an implicit relevance signal. Shared words create a comparative vocabulary. An abstract axis broad enough to include both phenomena is selected. Balanced sentences then make conceptual symmetry feel empirical. Later passages elaborate consequences from inside the new frame, so multiple reformulations look like independent confirmation. Finally, a local caveat labels the thesis provisional while the title, structure, and conclusion continue to treat it as the book’s organizing mechanism.

This last failure is particularly important. Uncertainty can appear in the text without exercising executive control over it. Words such as might, provisional, and unvalidated do not undo a firm title or a conclusion built around the disputed bridge. Once a formulation becomes a project’s skeleton, revision also acquires a second objective: preserving the manuscript. Objections are absorbed into refinements, new limitation sections, or more abstract versions of the same idea. The work protects continuity instead of reopening the original relational question.

Coherence pressure within the broader theory of smoothing

Smoothing is the broader hypothesis that transformations can attenuate decision-relevant contrasts among actors, evidence, causality, uncertainty, consequences, alternatives, and frames, making a message easier to accept or continue. Coherence pressure intersects with smoothing when an added bridge makes differences less accessible. Nothing must be deleted. A sufficiently memorable umbrella sentence can redistribute the entire text so that locally preserved distinctions no longer threaten its center. Perceived completeness rises faster than evidence-supported integration.

The relationship is limited. Euphemism, omission of agency, or compression under a length constraint can smooth without creating a common explanation. Conversely, a demonstrated synthesis can increase coherence while retaining distinctions and validity conditions. The book distinguishes reconstructive coherence, which reduces cognitive cost while preserving a path back; extractive coherence, which gains trust or closure by hiding the price; and ambiguous coherence, whose benefits and losses depend on purpose. A classroom metaphor, diplomatic compromise, scientific report, and fictional narrative cannot be judged by one identical standard.

From intuition to benchmark and intervention

CoherencePressureBench would compare cases with genuine common mechanisms, shared infrastructure but different local mechanisms, lexical similarity without relation, underidentified evidence, real normative conflict, and legitimate reconstructive synthesis. Outputs would be annotated for added relation types, support status, retained distinctions, compatible alternatives, and uncertainty propagation. Source-blind readers would rate coherence and sufficiency, while source-informed evaluators would judge relational fidelity. Decision effects—confidence, memory, information seeking, and experiment selection—matter more than whether a passage merely sounds too polished.

Candidate measures include unsupported bridge rate, distinction retention, compatible-answer coverage, evidence-supported integration, perceived coherence, and the gap between apparent unity and warranted integration. None is validated. Interventions include inventorying relations before synthesis, generating strong non-unified alternatives, requiring discriminating tests, maintaining a bridge ledger, using memory-free critics, and representing relations symbolically. Each may introduce new failures or suppress valid synthesis. The construct should be reduced or abandoned if annotations are unstable, effects vanish under controls, existing measures explain the variance, or caution destroys correct integration as readily as false closure.

Why read the complete book

A ten-minute synthesis cannot reproduce the evidential care with which the complete book positions the hypothesis among explanatory-coherence research, story models of judgment, processing fluency, autoregressive generation, decoding, preference tuning, sycophancy, false-premise behavior, confabulation, summarization, disagreement, and homogenization in co-writing. Those comparisons matter because the proposed construct may be redundant. The full text shows exactly which published findings support neighboring mechanisms and where the authors move into interpretation or technical conjecture.

The appendices are also essential to the book’s credibility. They preserve the original conversation, the over-unified manuscript fragments, the user’s trap, the retraction, and an autopsy of specific guilty sentences. A detailed experimental protocol specifies case classes, factorial prompts, training-stage comparisons, annotation passes, human-effects studies, statistical controls, adversarial negatives, and explicit falsification conditions. Extensions to SmoothingBench and SmoothTrace define provenance, challenge resistance, separation sensitivity, and relation invariants. Reading them reveals whether the proposed cure genuinely constrains storytelling or merely creates a more impressive story about having been wrong.
