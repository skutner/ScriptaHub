Large language models make natural language a practical interface to computation, but fluent prose remains an unstable execution surface. A model can silently change quantifier scope, merge identities, resolve an ambiguity, invent a bridge premise, or promote reported evidence into accepted fact before calling an exact solver. The solver may then calculate correctly over the wrong representation.
