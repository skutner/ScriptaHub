Rights Are Not Real

Power, Protection, and Why Morality Needs an Army

The easiest way to misunderstand rights is to assume that the word names a single thing. In ordinary speech it does useful moral work precisely because it compresses several different claims. We say that a person “has a right” and move quickly from moral entitlement to legal recognition to practical protection. In well-functioning systems the compression is often harmless because the layers reinforce one another.
