Why correction becomes the frontier

The book begins by reversing the usual promise of AI for science. Faster literature search, hypothesis generation, coding, simulation, and laboratory operation are valuable, but speed can also accelerate work through a contaminated benchmark, false causal model, or irrelevant conceptual space. AI makes candidates abundant before it makes decisive experiments, independent criticism, expert attention, or accountable authority equally abundant. The bottleneck moves from production to validation and judgment. The programme therefore defines science as a historically evolving social technology for making claims criticizable, not as a frozen checklist of procedures. A candidate becomes knowledge only by encountering resistance: prior work, counterexamples, severe tests, physical measurements, replication, and institutions capable of refusing release. This view rejects both mechanical scientism and romantic human exceptionalism. Machines may perform extensive scientific labour, but fluency, consensus, and benchmark improvement do not grant authority. The frontier is organized revisability—the capacity to locate error, preserve what it teaches, change representations, and expose the corrected claim to stronger tests.

Structural limits as design requirements

Several limits constrain the architecture. Finite evidence rarely selects one general model uniquely; unfamiliar data are not automatically novel; confidence is not calibration; correlations do not identify mechanisms; and distribution shift can invalidate an evaluator outside its tested conditions. An optimizing agent may also learn the test itself, converting an apparently neutral benchmark into part of the strategy. These are not reasons to abandon automation. They identify where uncertainty, assumptions, and decision rights must become explicit. The book distinguishes candidate abundance from validation scarcity and warns that a system can prepare persuasive work faster than communities can digest it. Stronger automation consequently needs stronger baselines, interventions, negative controls, regime-shift tests, and records of what could not be identified. It should be able to say when new sensors, experiments, or formulations are required rather than filling an information gap with confidence. The programme itself remains a portfolio of hypotheses: its concepts must compete with simpler tools and be abandoned or narrowed when they fail comparative tests.

From papers to evidence-carrying objects

The paper remains an effective public argument but a weak system of record for branching, computational, agentic inquiry. It compresses discarded hypotheses, failed controls, evolving assumptions, software environments, reviewer objections, and authorization decisions into a retrospective narrative. The book proposes an evidence-carrying research object: a versioned structure linking claims, specifications, datasets, code, experiments, negative results, provenance, permissions, and reasons for change. The paper becomes a signed view of that larger object rather than its sole durable form. Novelty within this system must be typed and relational. Absence from a model’s context, distance in embedding space, historical priority, mechanistic difference, and useful epistemic contribution are different claims requiring different memories and evaluators. Living specifications likewise preserve scientific intent without pretending to predict results. They can unfold a question into rival hypotheses, controls, budgets, stop conditions, and evidence obligations while recording unresolved ambiguity. Semantic versioning then reveals which changes affect meaning, not merely which bytes changed, and supports selective recomputation after correction.

Executable inquiry without a universal agent

Between scientific intention and heterogeneous tools, the book places a project-centred workbench. It maintains state over an inquiry’s lifetime, compiles approved specifications into bounded tasks, invokes adaptive and deterministic capabilities under explicit contracts, and recalculates only what a change invalidates. Crucially, it separates generation, search, evaluation, experiment, archiving, and promotion. A language model may propose equations, code, mechanisms, or protocols; an evaluator tests declared properties; an archive preserves diverse successes and informative failures; experiments supply contact with the world; and authorized actors change claim status. No node is sovereign. This design favours complex generators paired with simpler, independent verifiers wherever possible. It also allows plural representations: symbolic structures, statistical models, executable code, causal graphs, text, and laboratory records may each capture different aspects of an idea. Adoption should begin with the smallest structure that materially improves correction—stable identifiers, dependency graphs, reproducible environments, named validators, and review decisions—rather than maximal formalization that burdens researchers without increasing control.

Memory, critics, and evidential escalation

Scientific memory must preserve more than documents. A corrective system should remember how ideas were decomposed, why branches failed, what assumptions supported them, which transformations connect them, and what later evidence might revive an abandoned option. An operational idea atlas and project-level “consciousness” are proposed as inspectable memory functions, not artificial rulers. Criticism must be equally structured. Prompting the generator to critique itself does not create independence. Specialized critics should check formal validity, implementation, statistics, causality, robustness, novelty, domain relevance, governance, or compliance within declared scopes. Their initial judgments should be recorded before discussion so disagreement cannot disappear into a smooth synthesis. Evidence advances through stages: analogy, specified claim, executable artefact, independent reproduction, adversarial tests, specialist review, and external validation. This is not one universal truth score; requirements vary for a theorem, software method, clinical recommendation, or interpretive claim. Promotion and stopping require named decision rights. Capability to perform another action never by itself supplies authority to spend resources, expose data, release risks, or declare acceptance.

Different sciences provide different resistance

There is no universal scientific oracle. Proof assistants can certify formal steps; program tests can execute algorithms; instruments can measure physical systems; clinical trials operate under ethical and statistical constraints; archives and interpretive communities authorize different kinds of historical or social claims. The book searches for shared layers—proposal, evidence, evaluation, provenance, uncertainty, and authority—while preserving these domain differences. Mathematics and algorithm discovery permit adventurous generation because narrow checkers can sometimes provide strong local guarantees, although formalization may encode the wrong empirical problem. Equation discovery must survive units, symmetries, limits, interventions, and new regimes rather than merely fit sampled data. Autonomous laboratories require hard safety controls below open-ended agents, calibrated instruments, sample provenance, and evaluators outside the generation loop. Biology, chemistry, materials, clinical research, and social inquiry mix formal, empirical, causal, ethical, and interpretive resistance in different proportions. Even artificial scientific communities need independent data, anti-collusion design, costly contact with reality, and human legitimacy; machine consensus cannot authorize consequences for people.

Science is also power and political economy

The programme treats pedigree, ownership, infrastructure, geography, and labour as parts of methodology because they shape which questions receive attention and which failures remain visible. Reputation is a useful prior in an attention-constrained community, yet it can compound into immunity through grants, students, journals, networks, and instruments. Blind review or algorithmic ranking can merely replace visible pedigree with opaque model pedigree. More promising experiments create alternative routes to inspection, recorded dissent, transparent roles, appeals, and audits across different evaluators. Automation also risks deskilling researchers, obscuring invisible maintenance, and concentrating authority in platform owners. Privacy, copyright, consent, export controls, energy, data rights, and benefit sharing consequently belong inside the architecture. A layered scientific commons must permit exchange while retaining the ability to refuse, inspect, adapt, and challenge infrastructure. Human command cannot be a ceremonial click after agents have selected questions and spent resources. It must govern purposes, rights, limits, risk, credit, benefit, stopping, and appeal while allowing machines broad—but earned and reversible—delegation.

Why read the complete book

The complete work contains the reasoning that turns “correction” from a slogan into a research portfolio. Its philosophical history distinguishes severe testing from procedural ritual and explains why unoptimized variation can protect epistemic evolvability. Extensive technical surveys examine continual learning, causal discovery, symbolic regression, theorem proving, program search, autonomous laboratories, neuro-symbolic representations, privacy technologies, and machine scientific agents, always identifying guarantee boundaries and failure modes. The book also specifies demonstrators for research objects, living specifications, idea atlases, dependency fingerprints, validity dossiers, critic systems, compliance gates, and constitutional sandboxes, together with observations that should cause their revision or rejection. Examples across mathematics, software, biology, chemistry, materials, clinical work, and interpretive inquiry show why evaluator strength changes permissible autonomy. Finally, the discussions of pedigree, academic capitalism, authorship, scientific sovereignty, military competition, and infrastructure ownership reveal forms of power that a short conceptual account cannot fully trace. The value lies in seeing every proposed technology exposed to the same corrective discipline it asks science to adopt.
