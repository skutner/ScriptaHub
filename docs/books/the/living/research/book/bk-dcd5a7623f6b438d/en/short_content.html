Why the scientific paper is no longer enough

The book begins by separating science from one of its most successful interfaces. The research article solved genuine historical problems: it could be printed, transported, reviewed, catalogued, cited, and counted. Its compact structure created a workable agreement between authors, readers, journals, libraries, and career systems. That success made the article appear natural, even though it is better understood as an institutional technology shaped by scarce pages, costly distribution, limited reviewer time, and the need for administratively legible outputs.

Contemporary research produces a much larger object. Alongside prose and claims sit datasets, code, models, computational environments, protocols, calibration records, design choices, rejected approaches, negative results, ethical restrictions, licences, and tacit knowledge. These materials are now scattered across supplementary files, repositories, private directories, and departing researchers' memories. The article fronts this fragmented archive without adequately preserving how its pieces depend on one another. The book exists to ask what publication should become when that mismatch can no longer be treated as incidental.

AI reverses the economy of length

One plausible response is to publish less: compact claims, methods, datasets, and novelty statements from which AI reconstructs the necessary background. The book takes this atomic future seriously. Granular publication can establish priority earlier, give methods and negative results independent status, clarify contribution, and let criticism target one component without rejecting an entire paper. Machine-readable assertions and modular research objects address real weaknesses in the conventional all-in-one narrative.

Yet AI also makes the opposite future plausible. When extensive sources can be maintained cheaply and navigated selectively, a large record need not impose a large reading burden. One reader may request a five-paragraph contribution statement, another an executable tutorial, and a reviewer every claim depending on a disputed dataset. Source abundance becomes valuable when retrieval is traceable and grounded. The proposed settlement is therefore atomic underneath and monographic above: structured components for machines and coherent intellectual accounts for people.

The living book as a governed research system

A living research book is not merely a frequently edited digital volume. It is a versioned epistemic institution generated from a structured research environment. Its internal substrate identifies claims, evidence, methods, code, data, decisions, contributors, policies, and relations among them. A publication compiler selects authorized material, checks dependencies, applies privacy and intellectual-property rules, and produces an extensive human-facing edition. Stable releases remain citable even while a live branch continues to change.

The architecture separates three zones. A private workspace supports unfinished inquiry and candid disagreement. An evidence vault holds sensitive or controlled materials under explicit access conditions. The public book exposes what audiences need in order to understand and evaluate the work. A claim-evidence-provenance graph connects statements to supporting and opposing material, responsible contributors, transformations, and change history. The book can then offer several paths—conceptual, methodological, regulatory, pedagogical, or critical—without pretending every reader should proceed linearly.

Research becomes continuous knowledge construction

In this model, research and writing cease to be consecutive phases. Experiments, interpretations, literature changes, and rejected hypotheses update the maintained object as work proceeds. AI agents may monitor new sources, rerun examples, detect inconsistencies, propose mappings, preserve decision histories, and identify consequences of a changed assumption. Their role is maintenance and criticism, not sovereign authorship: consequential claims, releases, redactions, and judgments remain governed by accountable people and institutions.

The gain is not simply faster documentation. Negative results can remain visible instead of disappearing when they fail to support a publishable story. Practical knowledge—why a protocol repeatedly fails, which configuration is fragile, or which legal constraint redirected a design—can survive personnel turnover. A programme accumulates institutional memory rather than repeatedly reconstructing itself for each paper or report. Annual editions, funder reports, tutorials, and release notes become authorized projections of the same maintained knowledge base instead of separately rewritten narratives.

Review, authorship, and evaluation are reorganized

Reviewers currently spend substantial effort recovering context distributed across papers, repositories, and literature. The proposed reviewer cockpit starts from the epistemic delta: new, changed, corrected, contested, or retired claims and their dependencies. A reviewer can descend from a claim into evidence, methods, limitations, prior work, contribution records, and protected material. Controlled review capsules offer time-limited access to confidential data or code without exposing the entire private workspace.

Review can then become claim-specific, periodic, adversarial, and visible over time. The same structure also changes credit. Instead of deriving contribution from author order or publication counts, programmes can record conceptual work, software maintenance, replication, data stewardship, criticism, synthesis, and other roles. The author warns that fine-grained records can invite contribution inflation, managerial surveillance, metadata capture by senior figures, and new inequalities. Qualitative judgment, disputes, selected portfolios, and programme-level assessment must therefore constrain the apparent precision of the ledger.

Selective transparency and institutional risk

The living book integrates more of a project, so it must distinguish accountability from indiscriminate disclosure. Personal data, confidential testimony, security details, trade secrets, and pre-filing inventions may require protection. The public edition can still disclose data categories, collection logic, exclusions, governance, aggregate results, limitations, and what qualified reviewers inspected. Restrictions should be explicit, proportionate, time-bounded where possible, and sufficient to prevent privacy or commercial language from becoming a shield for unsupported conclusions.

The book also confronts its own failure modes. AI could build a cathedral of fluent emptiness whose scale and polish conceal a trivial contribution, or a coherent false world that integrates weak evidence too elegantly. Automated maintenance might narrow disagreement, introduce silent errors, or create dependency on expensive infrastructure. Defenses include contribution maps, frozen editions, independent retrieval, contradictory evidence, external review, provenance, human approval gates, and preservation systems. The proposal is a wager to be tested through pilots, not an inevitable destination for science.

Why read the complete book

This short account leaves out the monograph's detailed movement from existing standards and experiments—FAIR, RO-Crate, nanopublications, executable books, modular publishing, contribution taxonomies, and continuous reporting—toward a unified architecture. The complete book explains how workspaces, vaults, compilers, editions, citation, reviewer capsules, licences, patents, reporting systems, and career portfolios could interact. It also supplies a staged transition toward 2040 rather than presenting the finished institution without a route from current practice.

Most importantly, the full argument preserves qualifications that a compressed treatment can only name. It distinguishes machine readership from machine authority, accessibility from universal openness, extensive publication from archival dumping, contribution records from surveillance, and useful coherence from epistemic closure. Its imagined day in 2040 makes operational dependencies and breakdowns concrete, while its failure analysis tests the proposal against gaming, inequality, infrastructure capture, privacy theatre, and fluent fabrication. Those details determine whether the living research book is a credible research institution or merely an attractive metaphor.
